How it boots

No Android, no Linux, nothing emulated. Each stage hands the hardware to the next.

  1. S-BootSamsung's bootloader, unlocked. Loads our boot image from BOOT.
  2. UEFIProject Silicium's open starltePkg, with Carbon Hypervisor at EL2 handling PSCI.
  3. xnuloaderOur loader: moves the payload, fences off modem and DSP memory, and builds the device tree.
  4. XNUApple's open-source kernel with our patch and drivers, on GIC-400 and six cores.
  5. iOS 16.5launchd, then SpringBoard, from the system image on SYSTEM.

Install on a Galaxy S9

Why there's no download button. The installer contains iOS's own files, taken from an IPSW. We can't distribute those and neither can you, so you build the installer yourself. It's yours: don't share it.

You need

  • Galaxy S9 / S9+ / Note9, Exynos (G960F, G965F, N960F, FD models OK)
  • Unlocked bootloader and TWRP
  • A Mac (or Linux) with about 20 GB free, to build
  • The iOS 16.5 IPSW for iPhone10,3/10,6 (20F66), downloaded yourself from Apple

Not supported

  • Snapdragon and Korean (N) models
  • Apple ID, iCloud, App Store, iMessage
  • Anything that makes the phone pass as an iPhone to Apple

Steps

  1. Back up. In TWRP, back up Boot, System and EFS, and copy the backup off the phone. EFS holds your IMEI.
  2. Build on your computer, in one command (about 20 minutes and 20 GB of free disk): 
    git clone https://github.com/Scion-Project/scion-installer
cd scion-installer
./build-installer.sh --ipsw /path/to/iPhone10,3,iPhone10,6_16.5_20F66_Restore.ipsw
    Add --device star2lte, crownlte or all for the S9+ and Note9. It checks your IPSW, lists any missing tools with the command to install them (macOS: Xcode command-line tools + brew install sevenzip lld llvm bison meson ninja), and writes the zip and its SHA-256 to ./out. It downloads nothing from Apple. Details and troubleshooting.
  3. Flash. Copy the zip to a microSD card or to /sdcard with adb push (adb sideload can't take a zip over 2 GB), then in TWRP go to Install, pick the zip and swipe. It checks the model, writes SYSTEM and BOOT, and verifies both by SHA-256. It refuses to touch EFS, the modem or recovery.
  4. Reboot to System.

Leaving: hold five fingers on the screen for 1.5 s to restart into TWRP with the log, or force-restart with Vol Down + Power. Restore your Boot and System backups to get Android back.

iOS 27 (experimental, Galaxy S9 SM-G960F only). The installer above is iOS 16.5. iOS 27 also starts on the S9, from a kernel and a system image you build yourself from source and your own iPhone 11 restore image; there is no installer zip for it. It erases the phone, it has no Wi-Fi, sound, Bluetooth or cellular, and it can hang. The steps, what works and how to get back: iOS 27 on a Galaxy S9: build it and install it yourself.

FAQ

Is this an emulator?

No. On the S9 the XNU kernel runs directly on the phone's CPU cores, with drivers for its real hardware. Only the Pixel 10 route uses a hypervisor or emulator.

Can I use the App Store, iCloud or iMessage?

No, and that isn't a goal. Nothing here makes a phone pass as an iPhone to Apple's servers.

Why can't I just download an image?

An image contains iOS's own files, which only Apple may distribute. You build yours from an IPSW you get yourself, and it stays yours.

Can I go back to Android?

Yes: restore the Boot and System backups you took in TWRP. The installer never touches EFS, the modem or recovery.

Is the passcode real security?

Not yet. Partly. On the S9, keychain items are now encrypted under a key derived from your passcode, and forgetting the passcode loses them. Files aren't encrypted, there's no hardware-bound key yet so a short code can be guessed offline from a flash dump, and an unlocked bootloader lets anyone with the phone flash a modified kernel. Don't rely on it to protect serious secrets.

Does it work on the Snapdragon S9?

No. Only the Exynos 9810 models (G960F, G965F, N960F, and FD variants).

How can I help?

If you have an S9+ or a Note9, run the first test image and send us the log. Code, docs and driver reviews are welcome on GitHub.
